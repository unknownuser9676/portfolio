01 / THE MIND A STATEMENT OF INTENT

FIRST, A WAY OF THINKING

The known is a starting point, not a boundary.

I have never been particularly interested in accepting the limits of what already exists. I am drawn to the mechanisms behind things — the hidden structures, the underlying logic and the possibilities that emerge when seemingly unrelated ideas meet.

Technology gives me a language through which to explore those possibilities. Mathematics offers another. Design, science, architecture and art offer others.

I want to understand how things work, question why they work that way, and develop the ability to create alternatives.

I am not presenting a finished mind or a completed body of work. This is a record of a mind being developed through curiosity, experimentation and the discipline of making things.

01

Understand.

Go beneath the surface. Find the principles, mechanisms and systems that make things work.

02

Question.

Challenge assumptions. Examine limitations. Explore what conventional thinking overlooks.

03

Create.

Turn understanding and imagination into tangible experiments, systems and experiences.

04 / BEHIND THE WORK PROCESS / REASONING / DEVELOPMENT
CASE STUDY 001

Albanian Ice Cream

Giving an existing business a distinct visual identity.

01 — CONTEXT

A business is more than its product.

A customer doesn't experience a product in isolation. They encounter its name, visual identity, menu, presentation and personality. These elements influence how a business is recognised and remembered.

02 — THE DIRECTION

Heritage meets youthful expression.

The visual direction explored a meeting between premium, heritage-inspired branding and expressive contemporary graphics. The intention was to avoid an identity that felt either overly traditional or generic.

03 — MY ROLE

Turning direction into visual material.

I contributed to the logo, menu and visual exploration. The work gave me an opportunity to think about how design decisions relate to a real business and its customers.

04 — REFLECTION

Design has to function beyond appearance.

A visual identity must work in the everyday reality of a business. Recognition, clarity, consistency and practical use matter just as much as the initial visual impression.

CASE STUDY 002

The evolving portfolio

Building a digital environment rather than a template.

01 — THE QUESTION

Why should a portfolio behave like every other website?

Most portfolios follow familiar patterns. I wanted to explore a more personal approach where navigation, motion, visual composition and interaction contribute to the experience.

02 — THE APPROACH

Experiment with the medium itself.

The project uses web technologies to explore visual identity, interactive elements, responsive design and generative graphics. It is also an exercise in organising a growing body of work.

03 — THE DEVELOPMENT

A system that can evolve.

The portfolio is being developed as an ongoing project. Its structure and visual language will evolve as new experiments, projects and capabilities emerge.

04 — THE OBJECTIVE

Make exploration meaningful.

Visual spectacle alone is not enough. The experience should help visitors discover the thinking, process and work behind the person presenting it.

STATUS ACTIVE DEVELOPMENT
CASE STUDY 003

Stockroom

A product concept exploring simpler business management.

01 — THE OBSERVATION

Small businesses deal with complex everyday information.

Inventory and sales involve quantities, purchase costs, selling prices, stock movement and revenue. Keeping track of these details can become difficult without a clear system.

02 — THE IDEA

Make useful information easier to understand.

Stockroom explores the idea of a lightweight tool that could help a small business monitor inventory and understand its sales.

03 — THE PRODUCT QUESTIONS

What does the user actually need?

How should stock be recorded? What happens when products are sold? How can costs and revenue be presented clearly? Which calculations should happen automatically?

04 — THE NEXT CHALLENGE

Move from concept to a tested product.

The concept needs further development, interface decisions and practical testing before it can be presented as a functioning inventory management product.
